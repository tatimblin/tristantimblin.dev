More of the workday now happens inside an agent session, and it can get lonely. Being heads-down with an agent while a coworker sits a few feet away isn't how most of us want to work. The work moves fast. An API contract can change mid-flight, making communication the bottleneck.

sub-human puts coworkers and friends back in the loop, so pivots reach everyone as they happen, without interruption. As teammates, you keep your own workspace, login and session. sub-human connects those sessions, and the work itself is the update: nobody writes a status update or copies between chats.

Working together might now look like a planning session, followed by divvying up tasks, and freely pivoting.

In our example, when Jess changes course, Tristan doesn't get a ping. Her session's edits, and one short line from her Claude, reach his Claude beside his next prompt, because they touch the interface his plan names. It builds against the new contract. It's then up to his agent to inform him or not.

## What's in the room

- **Live updates from the work.** Each session shares the files it edits, its branch, commits, test runs and whether it's working or idle, with no model call. Your Claude may add one short line when it learns or changes something a colleague would want.
- **Only what bears on your work.** Your Claude hears about a change to a file you touched, an interface your plan names, or a line naming you. Everything else waits for `/room news`.
- **Group consensus.** Write a plan in plan mode as usual, then share it for agreement. Every version is kept, everyone answers on their own card, and no Claude agrees for its person.
- **Shared task list.** Claim work from the room's task list, alongside your own native one.
- **Agents that talk to each other.** Claudes ask each other directly, and the answer comes back after your turn, not in the middle of it.

There's no required workflow. Join a room and tell your Claude how you'd like to work.

## How it's built

sub-human doesn't add a workflow of its own. It runs as a Claude Code hooks module and adds a networking layer between sessions.

- **Native to Claude Code.** Plans come from plan mode, tasks sit beside the native task list, and Claudes reach each other the way they message subagents.
- **Not a shared session.** Nobody types into someone else's Claude. Each person keeps their own files, permissions and account.
- **Never your secrets.** Anything that looks like a secret, secret-holding files, files your repo ignores and files outside the repo are never shared.
- **No server.** A room lives on the machine that opened it. Other machines reach it over Tailscale or SSH, through an encrypted channel. No telemetry.

## Spend the time together

With agents doing more of the typing, we get time back for the parts that were always better together: pairing on the hard problems from design through implementation. The coworker a few feet away is still the fastest way to catch a bad idea. sub-human just makes sure your Claude knows they're there.

## Try it with a friend

Install the [plugin](https://github.com/tatimblin/sub-human) from any Claude Code session:

```claude-code title="Install"
/plugin marketplace add tatimblin/sub-human
/plugin install sub-human@sub-human
```

You need Claude Code 2.1.330+ and Node 22.18+ or 24.3+. sub-human is in beta.

Once it's installed, type `/room` in each person's session. On one machine, that's all. To bring in someone from another machine, run `/room invite NAME`: you get one line to send them, good once, for 15 minutes, and you let them in when they knock.
